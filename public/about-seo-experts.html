A foundation in scientific research

During a 1994 summer internship as a Physical Science Aid with the U.S. Geological Survey in Palo Alto, I conducted research for my Humboldt State senior thesis. I used cesium-137 (Cs-137) dating and X-radiography of sediment cores to investigate how quickly sediment accumulated in San Francisco Bay, comparing the evidence with the first appearance of an introduced species. The project strengthened habits I bring to SEO today: frame a question, compare evidence and explain what the data supports.

An idea from 2003

Why CyberFingers?

I chose the name in 2003 by bringing together two ideas: the Yellow Pages image of fingers doing the walking to find information, and “salt fingers” from my oceanography background.

Salt fingers are narrow streams formed as warmer, saltier water mixes with cooler, less salty water. “Cyber” carried those ideas into the early web era. The name connects discovery with my curiosity about how complex systems work.

Today, that idea also fits how I use AI: guide agents to gather information, connect sources and check what the evidence supports.
